## LBNL Pigment Database (this task, A4)

- **Database index:** https://coolcolors.lbl.gov/LBNL-Pigment-Database/database.html
- **Authors / attribution:** Ronnen Levinson, Paul Berdahl, Hashem Akbari —
  Heat Island Group, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL), Berkeley, CA.
  Contact listed on the site: RMLevinson@LBL.gov.
- **Reference publications** (the measurement/diffusion theory behind the datafiles):
  1. R. Levinson, P. Berdahl, H. Akbari (2005), "Solar spectral optical
     properties of pigments, Part I: Model for deriving scattering and
     absorption coefficients from transmittance and reflectance measurements",
     *Solar Energy Materials & Solar Cells*.
  2. R. Levinson, P. Berdahl, H. Akbari (2005), "Solar spectral optical
     properties of pigments, Part II: Survey of common colorants",
     *Solar Energy Materials & Solar Cells*.
- **License:** no explicit license is stated on the site; the database is
  published openly by a US national laboratory for research use. We cite the
  database and the two papers above wherever the data is used. The encrypted
  canonical datafiles additionally require a decryption key that LBNL issues
  only to Cool Colors project members (see "Encryption note" below) — we do
  **not** circumvent this; our spectra are digitized from the publicly
  accessible chart PDFs.
- **Local archive:** `model/datasets/raw/lbnl/` (see `MANIFEST.json` for
  sha256 of every file) → unified dataset `model/datasets/processed/pigments.json`.
- **Fetcher/digitizer:** `node scripts/fetch-lbnl.mjs all` (zero-dependency,
  idempotent; `--force` re-downloads).

### Encryption note (why we digitize charts)

Per `model/datasets/raw/lbnl/misc/spectral-datafile-guide.pdf`
(https://coolcolors.lbl.gov/LBNL-Pigment-Database/assets/misc/spectral-datafile-guide.pdf):

> "each spectral datafile is stored in the LBNL Pigment database as a ZIP
> archive with AES 128-bit encryption … Members and industrial partners of
> the Cool Colors project may obtain the decryption key via fax."

The chart PDFs are generated by LBNL from the same datafiles with one
polyline vertex per measured wavelength (300–2500 nm @ 5 nm), so vector
digitization recovers the published measurements without touching the
encryption. If the key is ever obtained, replace `processed/pigments.json`
arrays with the canonical text-file values (same sample ids; the zips are
already archived under `raw/lbnl/zips/`).

### Per-pigment URLs

URL patterns (verified live 2026-08-09):
- paint page: `https://coolcolors.lbl.gov/LBNL-Pigment-Database/paints/<CODE>.html`
- spectral data zip: `.../assets/spectral-data/<NAME>-spectral-data.zip` (AES-encrypted)
- spectral chart pdf: `.../assets/spectral-charts/pdf/<NAME>-spectral-chart.pdf`

`<NAME>` = `<CODE>-masstone` | `<CODE>-tint-1-to-4` | `<CODE>-tint-1-to-9`
(zips), `<CODE>-tint-ladder` | `<CODE>-masstone` (charts),
`<A>+<B>-mixture-1-to-1` (mixtures, both zip and chart).

**Substitution notes (PR254 / PR122 absent from LBNL):**
- *Red:* no PR254 Pyrrole Red exists in the database. R07 (PR209,
  3,10-dichloroquinacridone) is the closest clean organic red with a full
  tint ladder; tint hue h≈357 (D65/2°, from our data). R09 (PR9 naphthol,
  full ladder + 4 mixtures) is kept as an auxiliary red.
- *Magenta:* no PR122 exists. R08 (PV19 quinacridone, "Monastral Scarlet")
  is the only quinacridone rose/violet-chemistry paint; its masstone hue is
  scarlet-red (h≈21 over void) — an imperfect magenta proxy, flagged via
  `proxy_for`. LBNL has no better candidate (R06/PR206 is a maroon-orange).
- *Blue:* PB15 exists exactly (U12; plus second paint U13 masstone).
  PB29 ultramarine (U11, full ladder + 3 mixtures) included as the §3.1
  alternative.
- *White:* PW6 exists exactly (W03; plus second paint W04 masstone).

### Known limitations of the digitized LBNL data

1. K/S curves are only plotted (hence digitized) within 10^-1–10^3 mm^-1;
   outside → `null` in JSON (~8%/~11% of points). Measured reflectance and
   transmittance arrays are complete (300–2500 nm @ 5 nm).
2. Films are thin (≈10–37 µm); `reflectance` is film-over-void, not an
   opaque-layer spectrum. Use K/S (or derived `r_inf_km`) for K–M modeling.
3. Digitization quantization ≲0.001 reflectance; verified by R+T+A≡1
   (worst 1.5e-4) and cross-chart W03 reproducibility (RMSE 0).
